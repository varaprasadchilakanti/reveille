## What this is, in plain English

*This section is an explanation. It is not part of the agreement, and if it
ever disagrees with the agreement below, the agreement below wins.*

Reveille is maintained by one person. When you send a pull request, you are
handing over code that **you** own the copyright in. This document is how you
give the maintainer permission to use it.

**What you give up:** nothing you own. You keep the copyright in your
contribution. You can reuse your own code anywhere else, under any licence you
like, forever. This agreement is non-exclusive — it does not take your rights
away, it adds a permission on top of them.

**What you give:** permission. Specifically, permission broad enough that the
maintainer can distribute your contribution under the project's current licence
(Apache Licence 2.0) *and* under a different licence later, if the project ever
changes licence. That last part is the real reason this document exists — see
"Why a CLA at all" below.

**The floor you get in return:** whatever else happens, your contribution stays
available under the licence the project was using on the day you contributed
(Section 2.3). The maintainer cannot take your code proprietary-only and pull
the open-source version out from under you. He can add other licences; he
cannot subtract the one you contributed under.

**What is irrevocable, and what is not.** The permission you give is
irrevocable — you cannot withdraw it later, and neither can anyone who inherits
your copyright. That is not unusual: Apache-2.0's own copyright and patent
grants (§2 and §3) are irrevocable for the same reason. Code that could have
its licence pulled out from under it is code nobody can safely build on. What
*is* revocable is your participation: you can stop contributing at any time,
and this agreement asks nothing of you afterwards except Section 5.

Your acceptance also covers your **future** contributions to this project, so
you tick the box once rather than on every pull request. If you ever want a
particular contribution excluded, say so in that pull request.

**What this is NOT:**

- It is **not** a copyright assignment. You are not transferring ownership.
  (The Open Source Initiative distinguishes these two things directly:
  <https://opensource.org/faq> — "What are 'contributor agreements'?")
- It is **not** a claim on your other work, your day job, or anything you write
  outside this project.
- It does **not** oblige you to keep contributing, to provide support, or to fix
  anything later.
- It does **not** give the maintainer patent rights over anything except what
  your own contribution actually needs.

**Why a CLA at all.** Apache Licence 2.0 §5 already means that opening a pull
request licenses that pull request under Apache-2.0. So for today's purposes
this agreement is mostly belt-and-braces. It exists because §5 licenses your
contribution under *this* licence, and only this licence. If the project ever
needed to move to a different licence — a newer Apache version, a different
OSI-approved licence, or a dual-licensing arrangement — §5 alone would require
tracking down and getting agreement from every past contributor. This agreement
settles that in advance. The Linux Foundation lists exactly this as one of the
three standard reasons projects adopt a CLA:
<https://bestpractices.linuxfoundation.org/ip/contribution-mechanisms-cla.html>

**Honest disclosure.** A CLA is friction, and some contributors dislike them on
principle. That is a legitimate position. If you would rather not accept this
agreement, you are still welcome to file issues, report bugs, and discuss
design; and if you have a contribution you want to make on different terms, say
so in the pull request and it will be discussed on its own merits rather than
refused automatically.

### 6. How this Agreement is accepted

**6.1 Method of acceptance.** You accept this Agreement by doing **both** of the
following in connection with a pull request:

  (a) marking the contributor-agreement checkbox in the pull request body, in
      the form set out in the Project's pull request template, which states the
      version of this Agreement being accepted; **and**

  (b) including a `Signed-off-by:` trailer in the commits of that pull request,
      giving Your name and an email address, in the form produced by
      `git commit --signoff`.

Marking the checkbox is Your affirmative acceptance. The `Signed-off-by`
trailer identifies the person accepting and, separately, carries the
certification set out in the Developer Certificate of Origin, Version 1.1
(<https://developercertificate.org/>), which the Project also requires.

**6.2 Scope.** Acceptance covers the Contributions in the pull request in which
it is given and, unless You state otherwise in writing, Your future
Contributions to the Project as well.

**6.3 Consideration and mutual promises.** At Your desire and in
consideration of Your grants under Section 2, We promise that We will:

  (a) keep licensing Your Contribution under the Project Licence for as long as
      We distribute it, as set out in Section 2.3(b);

  (b) consider Your Contribution on its merits and give You a substantive
      response to it; and

  (c) if We include Your Contribution in the Project, preserve Your authorship
      of it in the Project's version control history, and not misattribute it to
      another person.

These are binding promises, not statements of intention, and We give them
because You have asked Us to. Together with Your grants they are reciprocal
promises forming the consideration for each other. The parties agree that this
consideration is sufficient, and neither party will contend that this Agreement
fails for want of consideration. The parties intend this Agreement to be legally
binding.

**6.4 Electronic acceptance.** The parties agree that acceptance given
electronically as described in Section 6.1 is valid and effective, and neither
party will dispute the validity of this Agreement solely on the ground that it
was formed by electronic means.

**6.5 Versioning.** This Agreement is versioned. The version that binds a
Contribution is the version identified in the checkbox You marked. Superseded versions remain in the Project's
repository at `docs/cla/` and in its Git history, so that it is always possible
to determine which text a given Contribution was made under. Changing this
Agreement does not retroactively change the terms of any earlier Contribution.

**6.6 If acceptance is not given.** If a Contribution is nevertheless merged
without acceptance having been given under Section 6.1, this Agreement does not
apply to that Contribution. Such a Contribution is licensed to Us under the
inbound terms of the Project Licence itself — for the Apache Licence 2.0, its
Section 5 — and under no broader terms. Nothing in this Agreement is intended
to reduce or displace that default.

### 7. Personal data

Accepting this Agreement does not require You to give Us any personal data
beyond what You have already chosen to publish yourself in Your commits and
pull request — that is, the name and email address in Your Git configuration
and Your platform account identity.

We do not maintain a separate register of contributor identities. The record of
Your acceptance is the pull request and the commit trailers themselves, held on
the hosting platform and in the Project's Git history.

If You do not wish Your email address to be public, configure Git to use a
platform-provided no-reply address before You commit. See
<https://docs.github.com/en/account-and-profile/reference/email-addresses-reference>.

Be aware, as the Developer Certificate of Origin also states, that a record of
Your contribution — including the personal information You submit with it,
including Your sign-off — is maintained indefinitely in the Project's version
control history and may be redistributed with the Project. Git history is
append-only by design, and rewriting it retrospectively is not generally
possible without breaking every existing clone. Please do not include personal
data in a Contribution that You would not want published permanently.

If You have a question, a request or a complaint about personal data in
connection with the Project, write to the address given in `PRIVACY.md`, which
also sets out what is held, why, and for how long. We will respond.
