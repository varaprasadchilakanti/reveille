# 0013 — "Who changes what" is opt-in, and organised by area, not by person

**Status:** Accepted (2026-10-09)

## Context

The question readers most often bring to a contributor list is not "who did the most" but "who
has worked on this part of the code": a reviewer looking for a second pair of eyes, someone new
to a codebase looking for the person to ask, a due-diligence reviewer asking whether a subsystem
has one author or several. The report answers none of it. The hotspot chart says which paths
change most and names nobody; the contributor table names people and says nothing about where
they worked.

The obvious answer is a sentence per person — "Alice mainly works on the adapters" — generated
by rules, the way the findings are. It was considered and rejected, for three reasons.

**It profiles a person.** A sentence describing what a named individual mainly does is a
characterisation of that individual, and it travels: it is the line pasted into a review or a
chat, without the chart or the caveat beside it. ADR 0010 made ranking opt-in because a default
that describes people is the measurement the project says it does not do; a per-person summary
is the same thing written in prose rather than in a score.

**"Mainly" is a judgement the data cannot support.** Commits touching a directory say where
someone's commits landed in a window, not what they know, own or are responsible for. A
maintainer who reviews everything and commits rarely would be described by the few places they
committed.

**The findings promise to name nobody.** "These sentences name no individual" is printed under
them, and that promise is worth more than a convenience.

Turning the question round keeps most of the value and loses most of the problem. Asked per area
— "who has changed `src/adapters/` in this window, and when was it last changed?" — the answer
describes the code, and people appear only as the list of those who touched it.

## Decision

**An opt-in section, `--area-authors` (`[areas] enabled = true`), lists the most-changed
directories, and for each one states how many commits changed it, how many authors made them,
who they are, and when the area was last changed.**

- **Off by default**, like the ranking, and not switched on by `--ranking`: every view that
  names people is a separate, deliberate choice. Without the flag nothing in the HTML, JSON or
  CSV changes beyond provenance recording that it was off.
- **An area is a directory of at most `--area-depth` components** (default 3, so
  `src/reveille/adapters` rather than `src/reveille`); a file in a shallower directory belongs to
  that directory, and files at the top level form an area of their own. Generated lock files are
  left out, as in the hotspot chart.
- **Areas are chosen by commits**, the most-changed eight. Choosing by lines would bring back
  generated churn; choosing by author count would select for the name lists.
- **Names are listed alphabetically, with no date or count per person.** One date belongs to the
  area: when it was last changed. Newest-first with a date per name was the first proposal and
  was rejected: it is a recency ranking under another name, and a person's last-commit date is
  read as a departure date.
- **Of more than five names, the five shown are those who changed the area most recently**,
  still in alphabetical order and still without dates, and the line says so ("the five to change
  it most recently, and N others"). The first five alphabetically was tried on a repository
  with 783 authors in one area and listed arbitrary handles; for "whom do I ask?" the recent
  five are the useful ones. Recency chooses who is shown and never orders them.
- **"Authors", not "people".** Identities are addresses (ADR 0002), one person can have several,
  and automation commits too. Identities with `[bot]` at the end of the name, or `[bot]@` in the address, are counted separately and
  listed on an "Automated" line.
- **Statements are factual and generated by fixed rules**: counts, one date, names. No "mainly",
  "owner", "expert" or share of an area per person. A list longer than five names ends "and N
  others". Two qualified statements are added where they apply: an area every commit of which
  came from one author ("one committer is not one maintainer") — only when the repository has
  more than one author, since otherwise it repeats under every area — and an area not changed in
  the last 30 days of the window.
- **The section is not part of the findings**, which keep their promise to name nobody. Its
  heading note says what it is not: who changed each area, not who knows or owns it.
- **ADR 0011 applies.** A contributor below `--min-commits` is counted among an area's authors
  and not named, and the line says how many are not listed. An excluded author is neither
  counted nor named.
- **JSON carries the same facts** under `areas` when the flag is on: the area, its commit and
  author counts, the date it was last changed, the listed authors (name and address), the
  automated ones, and how many are not listed. **No per-person count**, in JSON or HTML: it is
  the share of an area per person this record rules out, and a program summarising the JSON
  would state it. `schema_version` stays `1.1`, which had not shipped when this was written; the addition is additive
  (ADR 0008).

## Consequences

**The report can answer "who has changed this part of the code".** Under a flag whose name says
it adds names.

**The lists can be inverted, and that is stated rather than hidden.** A reader can count the
areas a name appears in, and in a repository with one or two authors each area's list is in
effect a profile. Nothing in the section does that counting, and no lookup from a person to their
areas is offered. As with ADR 0011's residual, a reader who should not see this should not be
given the report.

**An area's author count can reveal that an unlisted contributor worked there.** The line says
"N not listed" outright rather than leaving it as a subtraction, the same choice ADR 0011 made.

**It names people by area, so the page is personal data when the flag is used**, as the default
report's contributor table already is. `capabilities` and the README say so.

**A directory is a crude unit.** A monorepo's areas may be deeper and a flat repository's
shallower; `--area-depth` exists for that, and nothing tries to infer modules.

**The reader keeps one more aggregate while streaming**: per area, a commit count, a last date,
and each author's address with the date they last changed it — used only to choose which five
names a long list shows, and never printed or written out. Bounded by areas times authors, not by commits, which keeps the
property ADR 0004 and the file statistics rely on, and nothing is kept when the flag is off.

**It does not answer "who knows this code".** That needs blame across the surviving lines and
review history, neither of which Reveille reads (ADR 0005).
